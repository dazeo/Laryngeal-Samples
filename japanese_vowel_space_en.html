JAPANESE VOWEL SPACE (prototype)

F1 / F2 based on Yazawa & Kondo (2019), Japanese short-vowel formant averages (CC BY 4.0)

Trace the pad with your finger (or mouse) — the vowel sound changes with the F1/F2 at that position.

Vertical axis = F1 (tongue height — higher on the pad means a lower F1, i.e. a closer vowel). Horizontal axis = F2 (tongue front/back — further left means a higher F2, i.e. a fronter vowel).

All five Japanese vowels (i, e, a, o, u) are peripheral, so — unlike Malay — there is no central point like a schwa. The five points simply connect into a pentagon.
