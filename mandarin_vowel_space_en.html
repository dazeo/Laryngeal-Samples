MANDARIN CHINESE (BEIJING) VOWEL SPACE (prototype)

The six monophthongs of Standard Chinese: i (衣), ü/y (迂), u (乌), e/ɤ (鹅), o (喔), a (啊). Typical / representative F1-F2 values — see note below.

Trace the pad with your finger (or mouse) to hear the vowel change with F1/F2.

Vertical axis = F1 (tongue height). Horizontal axis = F2 (tongue front/back).

All six vowels are peripheral, so they are connected directly into a hexagon (no interior schwa-like point, unlike Malay). Note that /y/ (ü), a close front rounded vowel, sits between /i/ and /u/ in F2 — rounding pulls F2 down relative to unrounded /i/, even though the tongue position is nearly the same. /e/ here represents unrounded [ɤ] (as in 鹅), not the front vowel English speakers may expect from the letter "e" — its F2 is fairly central/back.
Note on the data: unlike the Japanese, Malay, and English pages in this set, these values are not taken from a single measured dataset. Two directly relevant peer-reviewed sources exist — Lee & Zee (2003), "Standard Chinese (Beijing)," Journal of the IPA 33(1) (Cambridge), and Zee & Lee (2001), "An acoustical analysis of the vowels in Beijing Mandarin," Proc. Eurospeech 2001 — but their formant tables are embedded as figures/images in the PDFs and could not be extracted as text in this environment. The values plotted here are typical/representative approximations (rounded, illustrative Hz figures consistent with how Standard Chinese monophthongs are usually described relative to one another) rather than numbers read directly off a specific published table. Treat this page as a structural/pedagogical illustration rather than a precise research-grade plot.
