KURMANJI KURDISH (BAHDINÎ) VOWEL SPACE (prototype)

Eight vowels, following Bijankhan & Saleh's (2017) acoustic study of Bahdini Kurmanji: five long/peripheral (î, ê, a, o, û) and three short/central (i, e, u — IPA ɨ, ə, ʉ). F1 values are real measurements from that study; F2 values are typical/representative — see note below.

Trace the pad with your finger (or mouse) to hear the vowel change with F1/F2.

Vertical axis = F1 (tongue height). Horizontal axis = F2 (tongue front/back).

The five long vowels (î, ê, a, o, û) are peripheral and connect into a pentagon. The three short vowels (i, e, u — IPA ɨ, ə, ʉ) are shown as smaller, lighter interior points: the study found them measurably more centralized (and shorter in duration) than their long counterparts, the same short-vowel-centralization pattern seen in several other languages in this set.
Note on the data: this page mixes two levels of reliability, and it's worth being precise about which is which. F1 values are real — extracted from Bijankhan, M. & Saleh, S. F. (2017), "Kurdish vowels – Bahdinan region acoustic research," Humanities Journal of University of Zakho 5(1), 1–9 (University of Zakho, Kurdistan Region–Iraq), based on Praat measurements of 552 recorded utterances from 23 speakers. F2 values could not be reliably extracted from that paper in this environment (the PDF's F2 table came through corrupted/scrambled in text extraction), so the F2 positions plotted here are typical/representative approximations based on the paper's own articulatory descriptions of each vowel (front/central/back, rounded/unrounded). This is, notably, one of the few pages in this set where genuine instrumental acoustic research on the language exists and is directly credited — worth naming given how much linguistic research on Kurdish has historically been suppressed or under-resourced. Treat the F1 axis as measurement-grounded and the F2 axis as structural illustration.
