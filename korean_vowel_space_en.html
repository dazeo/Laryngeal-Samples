KOREAN (SEOUL) VOWEL SPACE (prototype)

The eight monophthongs traditionally cited for Seoul Korean: i, e, ɛ, a, ɯ, ʌ, o, u. Typical / representative F1-F2 values — see note below.

Trace the pad with your finger (or mouse) to hear the vowel change with F1/F2.

Vertical axis = F1 (tongue height). Horizontal axis = F2 (tongue front/back).

All eight vowels are peripheral, so they connect directly into an octagon. Two things worth noting: /ɯ/ is a close back unrounded vowel — rare cross-linguistically — so its F2 sits higher (more central) than the rounded back vowels /o, u/ despite similar backness. And /e/ and /ɛ/, historically distinct, have merged for most speakers under 40 in real Seoul speech — they are kept separate here only because that is the traditional phonemic inventory; in practice many speakers produce them at nearly the same F1/F2.
Note on the data: as with the Mandarin and Cantonese pages in this set, these are not numbers read off one specific published table. Real measured data exists — Yang (1996), "Differences of vowel pronunciation system between Korean and American English," Journal of Phonetics, is the standard reference — but the formant tables in that paper are behind access barriers this environment could not get past. The values plotted here are typical/representative approximations, consistent with how Korean vowels are usually described relative to one another (cardinal-vowel-like corners, with the e/ɛ pair close together and /ɯ/ pulled toward the center for being unrounded). Treat this page as a structural/pedagogical illustration rather than a precise research-grade plot.
