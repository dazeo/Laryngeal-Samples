CANTONESE VOWEL SPACE (prototype)

The seven long-vowel monophthongs of (Hong Kong) Cantonese: i, y, ɛ, œ, a, ɔ, u. Typical / representative F1-F2 values — see note below.

Trace the pad with your finger (or mouse) to hear the vowel change with F1/F2.

Vertical axis = F1 (tongue height). Horizontal axis = F2 (tongue front/back).

All seven vowels are peripheral, so they connect directly into a heptagon (no interior schwa-like point). Note the two rounded/unrounded front-vowel pairs: /i/-/y/ (both close) and /ɛ/-/œ/ (both open-mid) — in each pair the rounded member has a noticeably lower F2 than its unrounded counterpart, even though tongue height and frontness are nearly the same.
Note on the data: as with the Mandarin page in this set, these are not numbers read off one specific published table. Real measured data exists — e.g. the "laryngeal" (typical-voice) comparison group in Ng, Gilbert & Lerman-style Cantonese alaryngeal-speech studies report F1/F2 for the 7 long vowels /i: y: ɛ: œ: a: ɔ: u:/ — but the formant tables in those papers are behind access barriers this environment could not get past. The values plotted here are typical/representative approximations, consistent with how Cantonese vowels are usually described relative to one another (cardinal-vowel-like corners, with rounding pulling F2 down for /y/ and /œ/). Treat this page as a structural/ pedagogical illustration rather than a precise research-grade plot.
