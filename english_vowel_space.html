ENGLISH (AMERICAN) VOWEL SPACE (prototype)

F1 / F2 from Hillenbrand, Getty, Clark & Wheeler (1995), "Acoustic characteristics of American English vowels," JASA 97(5). Pooled across men / women / children speakers (N-weighted means). 12 vowel categories — a much denser space than the 5-vowel Japanese or 6-vowel Malay systems.

Trace the pad with your finger (or mouse) to hear the vowel change with F1/F2.

Vertical axis = F1 (tongue height). Horizontal axis = F2 (tongue front/back).

The outer boundary (convex hull) connects only the peripheral vowels; interior points such as the diphthongs /eɪ/, /oʊ/ and the rhotic /ɝ/ fall inside the space rather than on its edge — shown here as separate, unconnected points.
