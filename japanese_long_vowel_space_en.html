In Japanese, vowel length is phonemic (e.g. obasan "aunt" vs. obaasan "grandmother"), but each long vowel is mainly the same vowel quality held longer, not a different vowel category — unlike English tense/lax pairs, which differ in quality (and are often diphthongized) regardless of duration. The measured data below shows this: each short/long pair sits close together on the pad, and the short-to-long shift (a few tens of Hz) is much smaller than the distance between two different vowel categories (a few hundred Hz). /a/–/aa/ shows the largest shift (F1 opens further), and /o/–/oo/ shows a modest fronting-vs-backing shift in F2, but neither pair crosses into another vowel's territory.
